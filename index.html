Wind

Picture yourself behind the rifle looking at the target, which is at 12 o'clock. Tap the dial where the wind is coming from. Only the sideways part of the wind counts here, so wind from straight ahead or straight behind adds no correction.

Wind changes downrange (optional)

Each extra wind replaces the one before it from its start distance outward. For example, 10 mph from your left out to 400 yd, then 5 mph from your left beyond that. Leave the speed blank to skip a wind.

    About and limits

    Estimates only. Dialed shows computed estimates. Check them with real drops at distance, and use the BC truing section to match your rifle. You are responsible for every shot, so always know your target and what is beyond it.

    What the math does and does not do:

    • It uses a point-mass model with standard G1 or G7 drag curves, not a measured curve for your bullet.
    • Wind is the crosswind part only. Aerodynamic jump is not modeled. Spin drift uses an approximation.
    • Coriolis only applies when you enter a latitude.

    Where the data comes from:

    • Bullet data is from the open ammolytics/projectiles dataset (MIT License), compiled from manufacturer specs in 2018. Newer bullets are missing and some numbers have been revised since. Check your bullet maker's current figure.
    • Weather and place lookups come from Open-Meteo. It is a model estimate for your area, not a reading at your firing line. A weather meter at the range is more accurate.
